									<h1 style="color: #202020 !important; display: block; font-family: Helvetica; font-size: 26px; font-style: normal; font-weight: bold; letter-spacing: normal; line-height: 26px; margin: 0; padding-bottom:10px; text-align: left"><span style="font-size:28px;"><span style="color:#3771c8;">Let's Encrypt&nbsp;</span></span></h1>

									<h3><span style="font-size:20px;"><span style="color:#003a70;">64-Day Certificate Lifetimes Coming Feb 2027</span></span></h3>
									<span style="font-size:16px;">On February 10, 2027, all Let’s Encrypt subscribers will move to certificates with 64-day lifetimes by default unless they select an even shorter lifetime (45 or 6 days, as previously announced). This means that any certificate we issue or renew on and after that date will have a 64-day validity period, and we expect the last 90-day certificate to expire on May 11, 2027. We will not revoke valid certificates as a part of this process.<br>
									<br>
									You can start testing in our <a href="https://outreach.abetterinternet.org/e/1011011/docs-staging-environment/4tgq2/%%email_id%%/h/%%internal.tracker_url_hash%%">staging environment</a>. Give it a try!<br>
									<br>
									Continue reading the full <a href="https://outreach.abetterinternet.org/e/1011011/2026-10-07-64-day-certs/4tgq5/%%email_id%%/h/%%internal.tracker_url_hash%%">blog post</a> to learn more.&nbsp;</span><br>
									&nbsp;
									<h3><span style="font-size:20px;"><span style="color:#003a70;">Two Additional Remote Perspectives for Domain Validation</span></span></h3>
									<span style="font-size:16px;">Let's Encrypt is adding two new remote perspectives for domain validation. This change is motivated by the fact that increased perspectives provide more domain validation security and is required by the <a href="https://outreach.abetterinternet.org/e/1011011/pective-issuance-corroboration/4tgq8/%%email_id%%/h/%%internal.tracker_url_hash%%">Baseline Requirements</a>. Increasing the number and coverage of our domain validation perspectives improves visibility and protection against BGP attacks.<br>
									<br>
									During domain validation, this means that we will make seven total validation requests, one from the primary datacenter and six from remote perspectives (<a href="https://outreach.abetterinternet.org/e/1011011/s-for-domain-validation-214123/4tgqg/%%email_id%%/h/%%internal.tracker_url_hash%%">previously</a> four). For validation to succeed, the primary server and a quorum of remote perspectives must receive the correct challenge response. This makes it more difficult for attackers to hijack validation requests. To learn more about multi-perspective domain validation, please <a href="https://outreach.abetterinternet.org/e/1011011/ti-perspective-validation-html/4tgqc/%%email_id%%/h/%%internal.tracker_url_hash%%">see our earlier blog post</a> when Let's Encrypt first added new perspectives.<br>
									<br>
									We expect little to no impact on users. Let's Encrypt began performing domain validation with the new regions in staging on Tuesday, October 27th, 2026. Assuming all goes well, we will rollout to production on Tuesday, November 10th, 2026.&nbsp;<br>

									<h3><span style="font-size:20px;"><span style="color:#003a70;">Bringing Divvi Up into the Post-Quantum Era</span></span></h3>
									<span style="font-size:16px;">Google and Cloudflare have set a goal of moving to post-quantum cryptography by 2029, which is sooner than the industry expected. That’s a strong signal that it’s time to prepare for quantum computers capable of breaking today’s encryption. In a new post, Divvi Up engineer Tim Geoghegan explains how we're getting ready. The cryptography Divvi Up uses to keep each person’s data private was already secure against quantum computers. The remaining work was in how that data is protected as it travels across the Internet. Divvi Up’s production systems now support post-quantum protections, and updated client libraries are on the way. Older clients will keep working, so users can upgrade at their own pace.<br>

									<span style="font-size:16px;">October 16 was a good day for the cryptography community.<br>
									<br>
									A 25-year-old question about the math behind some of the Internet’s most widely used encryption was finally <a href="https://outreach.abetterinternet.org/e/1011011/research-ecdsa-curve-origins/4tgr2/%%email_id%%/h/%%internal.tracker_url_hash%%">answered</a>. A group of engineers chipped in to fund it. And when a team of researchers at Anthropic solved it, they chose to donate the $36,864 prize to ISRG. We’re thrilled!&nbsp;<br>
									<br>
